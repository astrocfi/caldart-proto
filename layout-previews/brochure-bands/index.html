Volunteer pilots and aircraft for California's emergencies

CalDART organizes pilots, aircraft owners and ground crews into local teams at general aviation airports around the state. When an earthquake, wildfire or flood closes the roads, those teams fly relief supplies, responders and information to the communities that need them.

What CalDART does

Photo: Cessna 182 being loaded at Livermore

Pilots fly the missions

Private pilot certificate or higher, current medical or BasicMed, access to an aircraft. Flight time counts toward currency. Pilots without an aircraft are paired with owners in their DART.

Aircraft owners: single-engine and light twin aircraft based at a member airport.

Photo: ground crew at the Hayward workshop

Ground crews move the load

No pilot certificate needed. Loading, radio, logistics, airport liaison. Ground volunteers train with their DART through the year and work the ramp on an activation, handing each load off to the agency that asked for it.

Emergency managers request support

County OES, hospitals, tribal governments and mutual-aid partners request support through the duty coordinator. Eleven counties hold a signed agreement with CalDART.

Requests for air support: call the duty coordinator at (555) 010-2201, any hour. Give the agency name, the mission, the departure and arrival airports and the deadline.

Our mission

CalDART gives California's emergency managers a trained, insured and practiced volunteer air transportation capability. Teams are organized, exercised and kept current all year, so they are ready on the day an agency calls.

How an activation works

StepWho actsWhat happens
1. Local teams form around an airport Pilots, aircraft owners, ground volunteers Each DART is a group of pilots, aircraft owners and ground volunteers based at one general aviation airport and led by a DART coordinator.
2. An agency requests air support County EOC or partner agency A county emergency operations center or a partner agency contacts CalDART's duty coordinator with the mission: what, where and by when.
3. The coordinator matches the mission to aircraft CalDART duty coordinator Available aircraft and pilots are matched to the load, the distance and conditions at the destination airport.
4. Volunteers fly the mission Pilots and ground crews Pilots fly at their own discretion and expense under standard FAA rules. Ground crews load, unload and hand off the cargo.
5. Every flight is logged DART coordinator Each leg is recorded for the agency's records and for CalDART's own after-action review.

Questions

Do I need my own aircraft?

No. Pilots without an aircraft are paired with owners in their DART.

Who pays for the flight?

The pilot, unless an agency agreement says otherwise. The cost is generally deductible as a charitable expense.

Is there insurance?

CalDART carries non-owned aircraft liability coverage for missions it dispatches. Owners keep their own hull and liability policies.

What does a DART do between emergencies?

Trains, exercises with county agencies and keeps its roster and aircraft list current.
