Volunteer pilots and aircraft for California's emergencies

CalDART organizes pilots, aircraft owners and ground crews into local teams at general aviation airports around the state. When an earthquake, wildfire or flood closes the roads, those teams fly relief supplies, responders and information to the communities that need them.

For pilots, owners and ground crew

  • Pilots. Private pilot certificate or higher, current medical or BasicMed, access to an aircraft. Flight time counts toward currency.
  • Aircraft owners. Single-engine and light twin aircraft based at a member airport.
  • Ground crew. No pilot certificate needed. Loading, radio, logistics, airport liaison.

How to request support

How an activation works

CalDART gives California's emergency managers a trained, insured and practiced volunteer air transportation capability. Teams are organized, exercised and kept current all year, so they are ready on the day an agency calls.

Local teams form around an airport

Each DART — Disaster Airlift Response Team — is a group of pilots, aircraft owners and ground volunteers based at one general aviation airport and led by a DART coordinator.

An agency requests air support

A county emergency operations center or a partner agency contacts CalDART's duty coordinator with the mission: what, where and by when.

The coordinator matches the mission to aircraft

Available aircraft and pilots are matched to the load, the distance and conditions at the destination airport.

Volunteers fly the mission

Pilots fly at their own discretion and expense under standard FAA rules. Ground crews load, unload and hand off the cargo.

Every flight is logged

Each leg is recorded for the agency's records and for CalDART's own after-action review.
