Notice to emergency managers

(555) 010-2201

Requests for air support go to the CalDART duty coordinator at the number above, any hour. Give the agency name, the mission, the departure and arrival airports and the deadline, and the coordinator will match the mission to available aircraft and pilots.

Volunteer pilots and aircraft for California's emergencies

CalDART organizes pilots, aircraft owners and ground crews into local teams at general aviation airports around the state. When an earthquake, wildfire or flood closes the roads, those teams fly relief supplies, responders and information to the communities that need them.

Key facts

Summary of CalDART's organization, service area and membership.
Who we are CalDART, the California DART Network. A DART is a Disaster Airlift Response Team: pilots, aircraft owners and ground volunteers based at one general aviation airport and led by a DART coordinator. 15 DARTs, 240 members and 96 aircraft.
What we do CalDART gives California's emergency managers a trained, insured and practiced volunteer air transportation capability. Teams are organized, exercised and kept current all year, so they are ready on the day an agency calls. 3 activations and 6 exercises in the past 12 months.
Where we operate General aviation airports from Santa Rosa to Santa Monica, grouped into six regions: North Bay, East Bay, Peninsula, South Bay, Central Coast and Southern California. 11 counties have a signed agreement. See the list of DARTs.
How to request support County OES, hospitals, tribal governments and mutual-aid partners call the duty coordinator at (555) 010-2201, any hour.
How to join Pilots need a private pilot certificate or higher, a current medical or BasicMed and access to an aircraft; flight time counts toward currency. Aircraft owners bring single-engine or light twin aircraft based at a member airport. Ground crew need no pilot certificate: loading, radio, logistics and airport liaison. Join CalDART.
Tax status 501(c)(3) public charity, EIN 00-0000000. Dues and contributions are tax deductible to the extent allowed by law. Pilots pay for their own flights, unless an agency agreement says otherwise, and the cost is generally deductible as a charitable expense.

How an activation works

  1. Local teams form around an airport. Each DART is a group of pilots, aircraft owners and ground volunteers based at one general aviation airport and led by a DART coordinator.
  2. An agency requests air support. A county emergency operations center or a partner agency contacts CalDART's duty coordinator with the mission: what, where and by when.
  3. The coordinator matches the mission to aircraft. Available aircraft and pilots are matched to the load, the distance and conditions at the destination airport.
  4. Volunteers fly the mission. Pilots fly at their own discretion and expense under standard FAA rules. Ground crews load, unload and hand off the cargo.
  5. Every flight is logged. Each leg is recorded for the agency's records and for CalDART's own after-action review.

Before you call: activation checklist

Have these ready when you reach the duty coordinator. They are what the coordinator needs to start matching aircraft to the mission.

  • Agency name and the requesting officer, with a callback number.
  • The mission: what is being moved, how much it weighs and how many people travel with it.
  • Departure and arrival airports, and any restriction in force at either one.
  • The deadline: when the load must arrive, and when it can first be collected.
  • A ground contact at each end who will meet the aircraft and sign for the load.
