Volunteer pilots and aircraft for California's emergencies

Volunteer disaster air transportation for California

Agency requests, any hour: (555) 010-2201

CalDART organizes pilots, aircraft owners and ground crews into local teams — Disaster Airlift Response Teams, or DARTs — at general aviation airports around the state. When an earthquake, wildfire or flood closes the roads, those teams fly relief supplies, responders and information to the communities that need them.

CalDART gives California's emergency managers a trained, insured and practiced volunteer air transportation capability. Teams are organized, exercised and kept current all year, so they are ready on the day an agency calls.

Who joins. Pilots hold a private pilot certificate or higher, a current medical or BasicMed, and access to an aircraft; flight time counts toward currency. Aircraft owners bring single-engine and light twin aircraft based at a member airport. Ground crew need no pilot certificate: loading, radio, logistics and airport liaison.

Emergency managers. County OES, hospitals, tribal governments and mutual-aid partners request support through the duty coordinator. Give the agency name, the mission, the departure and arrival airports and the deadline.

Membership. Annual membership is $45 a year, renewable each year. Life membership is $650, paid once. A Friend of CalDART gives a donation of any amount, with no membership. Already a member? Sign in to the member portal to update your profile, check your medical and insurance dates, or renew.

Do I need my own aircraft? No. Pilots without an aircraft are paired with owners in their DART.

Who pays for the flight? The pilot, unless an agency agreement says otherwise. The cost is generally deductible as a charitable expense.

Is there insurance? CalDART carries non-owned aircraft liability coverage for missions it dispatches. Owners keep their own hull and liability policies.

What does a DART do between emergencies? Trains, exercises with county agencies and keeps its roster and aircraft list current.

How an activation works

  1. Local teams form around an airport. Each DART is a group of pilots, aircraft owners and ground volunteers based at one general aviation airport and led by a DART coordinator.
  2. An agency requests air support. A county emergency operations center or a partner agency contacts CalDART's duty coordinator with the mission: what, where and by when.
  3. The coordinator matches the mission to aircraft. Available aircraft and pilots are matched to the load, the distance and conditions at the destination airport.
  4. Volunteers fly the mission. Pilots fly at their own discretion and expense under standard FAA rules. Ground crews load, unload and hand off the cargo.
  5. Every flight is logged. Each leg is recorded for the agency's records and for CalDART's own after-action review.
