Who CalDART is for

Photo: pilot and ground volunteer loading a Cessna 182 at Livermore

Pilots

CalDART organizes pilots, aircraft owners and ground crews into local teams at general aviation airports around the state. You fly at your own discretion and expense under standard FAA rules, and the flight time counts toward currency. Pilots without an aircraft are paired with owners in their DART.

  • Private pilot certificate or higher
  • Current medical or BasicMed
  • Access to an aircraft, or a pairing with an owner at your airport
  • Annual membership, $45 a year

Ground crew

No pilot certificate is needed. Ground volunteers load and unload aircraft, work the radios, run the logistics at each end of a mission and act as the liaison with airport management. Between emergencies a DART trains, exercises with county agencies and keeps its roster and aircraft list current.

  • Loading and unloading, and the paperwork that travels with a load
  • Radio and communications
  • Logistics and airport liaison
  • Annual membership, $45 a year
Join
Photo: Cessna 182 on the ramp at Reid-Hillview

Aircraft owners

CalDART flies single-engine and light twin aircraft based at a member airport. CalDART carries non-owned aircraft liability coverage for the missions it dispatches; owners keep their own hull and liability policies. An owner who does not want to fly a mission can pair the aircraft with a pilot in the same DART.

  • Single-engine or light twin aircraft based at a member airport
  • Your own hull and liability policies, kept current
  • A current aircraft record in the member portal
  • Annual membership, $45 a year

Donors

Volunteers fly at their own expense. Donations pay for the insurance, the training and the exercises that keep fifteen teams current between emergencies. A Friend of CalDART gives any amount and takes no membership; members can also pay once and never renew.

  • Annual membership, $45 a year, renewable each year
  • Life membership, $650, paid once
  • Friend of CalDART, a donation of any amount

About CalDART

CalDART organizes pilots, aircraft owners and ground crews into local teams at general aviation airports around the state. A DART is a Disaster Airlift Response Team. When an earthquake, wildfire or flood closes the roads, those teams fly relief supplies, responders and information to the communities that need them.

CalDART gives California's emergency managers a trained, insured and practiced volunteer air transportation capability. Teams are organized, exercised and kept current all year, so they are ready on the day an agency calls. There are 15 DARTs, 240 members and 96 aircraft, and 11 counties hold a signed agreement.

15 DARTs, Santa Rosa to Santa Monica

How an activation works

Local teams form around an airport

Each DART is a group of pilots, aircraft owners and ground volunteers based at one general aviation airport and led by a DART coordinator.

An agency requests air support

A county emergency operations center or a partner agency contacts CalDART's duty coordinator with the mission: what, where and by when.

The coordinator matches the mission to aircraft

Available aircraft and pilots are matched to the load, the distance and conditions at the destination airport.

Volunteers fly the mission

Pilots fly at their own discretion and expense under standard FAA rules. Ground crews load, unload and hand off the cargo.

Every flight is logged

Each leg is recorded for the agency's records and for CalDART's own after-action review.
