## Headline and lede

Headline: Volunteer pilots and aircraft for California's emergencies

Lede: CalDART organizes pilots, aircraft owners and ground crews into local teams at
general aviation airports around the state. When an earthquake, wildfire or flood
closes the roads, those teams fly relief supplies, responders and information to the
communities that need them.

## Mission

CalDART gives California's emergency managers a trained, insured and practiced
volunteer air transportation capability. Teams are organized, exercised and kept
current all year, so they are ready on the day an agency calls.

## How an activation works (five steps)

1. Local teams form around an airport. Each DART is a group of pilots, aircraft
   owners and ground volunteers based at one general aviation airport and led by a
   DART coordinator.
2. An agency requests air support. A county emergency operations center or a partner
   agency contacts CalDART's duty coordinator with the mission: what, where and by
   when.
3. The coordinator matches the mission to aircraft. Available aircraft and pilots are
   matched to the load, the distance and conditions at the destination airport.
4. Volunteers fly the mission. Pilots fly at their own discretion and expense under
   standard FAA rules. Ground crews load, unload and hand off the cargo.
5. Every flight is logged. Each leg is recorded for the agency's records and for
   CalDART's own after-action review.

## Who we serve / who joins

- Pilots: private pilot certificate or higher, current medical or BasicMed, access to
  an aircraft. Flight time counts toward currency.
- Aircraft owners: single-engine and light twin aircraft based at a member airport.
- Ground crew: no pilot certificate needed. Loading, radio, logistics, airport
  liaison.
- Emergency managers: county OES, hospitals, tribal governments and mutual-aid
  partners. Request support through the duty coordinator.

## Frequently asked questions

- Do I need my own aircraft? No. Pilots without an aircraft are paired with owners in
  their DART.
- Who pays for the flight? The pilot, unless an agency agreement says otherwise. The
  cost is generally deductible as a charitable expense.
- Is there insurance? CalDART carries non-owned aircraft liability coverage for
  missions it dispatches. Owners keep their own hull and liability policies.
- What does a DART do between emergencies? Trains, exercises with county agencies and
  keeps its roster and aircraft list current.
