===========
My aircraft
===========

**My aircraft** lists the airplanes you commonly fly. A DART leader checks this
list, and each airplane's insurance, before deciding whether to launch you on a
mission, so keep it current.

CalDART keeps one shared list of airplanes for everybody. When you add an airplane
here, CalDART links its record on that list to your profile. You don't get a
private copy. Open the screen from **My aircraft** in the menu, from the **My aircraft** link at
the top of :doc:`profile`, or from **Add the planes I fly** at the end of :doc:`join`.
**Back to My profile**, a link at the top right, returns to :doc:`profile`.

**My aircraft** is for members with a current membership. A friend of CalDART
doesn't have **My aircraft** in the menu or any aircraft on their profile, and a new
member doesn't have it until their first dues are paid. If your membership expires,
the airplanes stay on your profile. Until you renew, though, the screen shows
*Aircraft are for members with a current membership.* instead of them. Once you
renew, they are back.


What you see
============

If CalDART's coverage policy has a note for members, such as *Helicopters are not
covered*, it appears at the top of the **Your aircraft** card. The note says which
airplanes CalDART's insurance doesn't cover. A DART leader treats an airplane outside
that coverage as a no-go.

The **Your aircraft** card lists each airplane on your profile: its N-number, make,
and model, its insurance status with the expiry date on file, and the liability
limits, with each limit named, for example *Liability $1,000,000 per occurrence,
$100,000 per person*. The status uses the same words as the search results below and
every other aircraft list:

* **Insured to 03/01/2027**: a policy is on file and has more than 30 days to run;
* **Expiring 10/31/2026**: it runs out within 30 days;
* **Expired 03/02/2026**: the expiry date on file has passed;
* **No insurance on file**: the record doesn't have an insurance expiry date at all.

After the status comes the insurance's mark. It says **Verified** once a DART leader
or a verifier has checked the policy against its documents, and **Not yet verified**
until then. If the status is **No insurance on file**, there isn't a mark, because
there isn't a policy to verify yet. A DART leader still treats that airplane as a
no-go, and a verifier still sees its insurance as not verified. A DART leader treats
any airplane with unverified insurance as a no-go.

If the coverage policy leaves an airplane out, it shows **Not covered** beside a red
dot, and the reason comes after the limits, such as *Not covered: helicopters are
excluded by CalDART's policy*.

If you haven't added any airplanes, the card says **No aircraft yet**.

Each airplane you added yourself has **Edit** and a trashcan, **Remove**, beside it.
An airplane somebody else added doesn't have **Edit**. Instead, its line says *Added
by someone else. To correct it, write to* followed by the site's contact address.
Clicking the address opens your email program (see *Editing an airplane* below).

Below that card, the **Find an aircraft** card searches CalDART's aircraft list.


Adding an airplane you fly
==========================

#. Type in **Search CalDART's aircraft list**: the N-number, or the make, model, or
   owner if you don't have the number handy.
#. The results fill the card's width. They say *Choose an aircraft to add it to your
   list.*, or, if the search found just one, *Press Enter, or choose it, to add it to
   your list.* Each result shows its N-number, make and model, its insurance status
   (**Insured**, **Expiring soon**, **Insurance expired**, or **No insurance on
   file**), and **Out of service** for an airplane taken out of use.
#. Click the airplane, or press Enter in the search box when it is the only result.
   It is added straight away, *N12345 added.* appears, and the airplane joins the
   list above. The screen takes you to its line.

You can type a registration however you like. CalDART writes every N-number one
way: upper case, no punctuation, and an N at the front. So 12345, n12345, and
N-12345 all find N12345, and an airplane can only be on CalDART's aircraft list once.

The results leave out airplanes already on your list, and name them underneath
instead, such as *N12345 is already on your list.* or *N12345 and N9021K are already
on your list.* If the search doesn't find anything, it says **No aircraft matches
that**. The results also leave out an airplane taken out of service, unless you type
its exact registration, so you don't add it twice.


Adding an airplane that is not on CalDART's list
================================================

**Add a new aircraft** is at the bottom of the card (*Not on CalDART's list? Add it
yourself.*), and you don't have to search first. It opens **Add an aircraft to the
register** where the button was, and puts the cursor in the first box. **Cancel**, or
Escape, closes it. The form has every box the record has, the same ones you see when
you edit it later:

* **N-number** (required). The box adds the N for you and takes digits first, then
  at most two letters, so 172sp becomes N172SP. It starts empty, whatever you
  searched for, so type the N-number here. As you type, a list under the box shows
  airplanes from the FAA data whose N-number starts with what you typed: see
  :ref:`registry-list`.
* **Year**, four digits.
* **Aircraft type** (required): type the make, the model, or a designator, for example
  cessna 172, c172, or skyhawk, and pick the type from the list under the box. Each
  entry shows a make and model, followed by its seats. You can only set the type by
  picking from the list, so each type is always written the same way, however people
  type it. If the search doesn't find anything, it says *No aircraft type matches
  that.*
* **Seats**. If **Seats** is empty, picking a type fills it in.
* **Category**, such as Airplane, Helicopter, Gyroplane, Glider, or Balloon, and
  **Airworthiness**, the category on the airworthiness certificate, such as Standard,
  Experimental, or Light sport. Either can stay **Not recorded**. But if **Category**
  isn't recorded, a DART leader's aircraft check says *Category not recorded*,
  whatever the airworthiness. If the registry knows a type's category, picking that
  type fills in **Category**.
* **Owner type** (Individual, FBO, or Flying club), **Owner name**, and **Owner
  contact**, an email address or a phone number. If the owner is an individual and
  you type the name all in capitals or all in lower case, it is saved in title case,
  like your own name (see :doc:`profile`). An FBO's or a club's name is saved as typed.
* **Carrier**, **Policy number**, **Liability per occurrence**, **Liability per
  person**, **Hull**, and **Insurance expires**. Amounts are in US dollars, and
  the commas fill in as you type. **Insurance expires** is the most important box on
  the form, because a DART leader looks at it.

Press **Add aircraft**. The airplane joins CalDART's aircraft list and your list at once.
**Cancel** closes the form.

.. _registry-list:

Picking an airplane from the FAA data
-----------------------------------------

Every US airplane is in the FAA's aircraft registry, and CalDART keeps a copy of it,
updated every night. Type the start of the N-number, such as N17, and a list under
the box shows up to eight airplanes whose N-number starts with it. Each one shows
its N-number, aircraft type, year, and registrant. Keep typing to narrow the list.
Click one, or move to it with the arrow keys and press Enter. Escape closes the list.

When you pick an airplane, its N-number goes into the box, and the form fills in its
aircraft type, year, seats, category, and airworthiness. **Owner name** gets the
registrant's name. **Owner type** is a guess from the kind of registrant: a person
or co-owners become **Individual**, a partnership becomes **Flying club**, and a
company becomes **FBO**. For a government registrant, or any other kind, **Owner
type** stays as it was. The line under the N-number box then says, for example,
*From FAA data as of 09/20/2026*, the date of CalDART's copy. It stays until you
change the N-number again. Check what the form filled in, and correct anything out
of date.

If the registry doesn't have the airplane, it doesn't appear in the list. Finish
typing its N-number and fill in the rest of the form by hand.

If CalDART's aircraft list already has the airplane, the form says so as soon as the
N-number is in the box, picked or typed, before you fill in anything else. It says
*N30075 is already on file.*, with an **Add it to my list** button that adds that
record to your list and closes the form. If the airplane is already on your list, it
says *N30075 is already on your list.* While that line shows, the list of FAA
airplanes under the box stays closed.


Editing an airplane
===================

Press **Edit** beside an airplane. The form opens right under that airplane, and the
rest of your list continues below it. Escape, **Cancel**, or **Close** (which takes
the place of **Edit**) closes the form again. Saving closes it too and takes you back
to **Edit**. For an airplane you added yourself, the form opens with every detail as
it was added: **N-number**, **Year**, **Aircraft type**, **Seats**,
**Category**, and **Airworthiness**; **Owner type** (Individual, FBO, or Flying club), **Owner name**, and
**Owner contact**; and **Carrier**, **Policy number**, **Liability per occurrence**,
**Liability per person**, **Hull**, and **Insurance expires**. Amounts are in US
dollars, and the commas fill in as you type. A limit that was never recorded shows
as an empty box. To change the type, type in **Aircraft type** and pick the new one
from the list. If **Seats** is empty, picking a type fills it in. Typing in
**N-number** lists the registry's airplanes again, and picking one fills the form
from it (:ref:`registry-list`). Press **Save changes**, and *N12345 updated.*
appears. If you change any insurance detail, the insurance loses its verification
when you save. The mark says **Not yet verified** until a DART leader or a verifier
checks the new policy.

An airplane somebody else added doesn't have **Edit**. Its line asks you to write to
the site's contact address instead. The list is shared, so flying an airplane
doesn't let you change its record. An account administrator is the exception. For
them, **Edit** opens any airplane on the list.


Removing an airplane
====================

The trashcan asks before it removes anything. Press it, and it turns into **Remove**
and **Cancel**, alone on the line. **Edit**, or **Close**, is hidden until you
choose. Press **Remove**, and the airplane comes off your list and *N12345 removed.*
appears. If you press **Cancel**, press Escape, or click somewhere else, nothing
changes. Removing only takes the airplane off your list. The record stays on
CalDART's aircraft list, and anyone else who flies it keeps it on their list. Only an
account administrator can delete an airplane from CalDART's aircraft list.


If something looks wrong
========================

*Pick the aircraft type from the list.* means you typed in the **Aircraft type** box
but didn't pick a type from the list. If the list doesn't have an entry for your
airplane, ask an account administrator to add its type. *An aircraft with this
N-number is already on file.* means CalDART's aircraft list already has it. Press
**Add it to my list** under the N-number, or search for it and add that record.
*Use a US registration like N172SP: N, then digits, then at most two letters.* means
what you typed isn't a valid US registration. *Enter an amount of $0 or more.* means
a dollar box has something in it that isn't an amount. If the insurance status says
**No insurance on file** and the airplane is insured, the record is missing the
expiry date. If you added the airplane, add the date. If not, write to the contact
address on its line.
